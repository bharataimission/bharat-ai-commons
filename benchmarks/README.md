# Benchmarks and Evaluation

## Purpose

Bharat AI Commons aims to support transparent, reproducible evaluation of AI systems for Indian languages and Indian contexts.

Model availability alone does not establish model quality. Evaluation can help researchers, developers, students, and organisations understand how systems perform across languages, tasks, and real-world use cases.

This directory will document evaluation methodologies, benchmark resources, reproducible experiments, and findings as the initiative develops.

## Initial Focus Areas

### 1. Indian-Language Understanding

Explore evaluation tasks involving text classification, question answering, summarisation, and other language understanding capabilities.

### 2. Translation

Document evaluation approaches for translation between Indian languages and between Indian languages and other languages.

### 3. Speech Technologies

Explore speech recognition and text-to-speech evaluation, including language coverage and variation in speech.

### 4. Contextual and Cultural Relevance

Identify ways to assess whether AI systems appropriately handle Indian names, places, cultural references, and locally relevant contexts.

### 5. Robustness and Responsible AI

Document relevant limitations, failure cases, fairness considerations, and risks associated with evaluation datasets and model outputs.

## Evaluation Principles

Every evaluation initiative should aim to:

1. **Be reproducible:** Document the dataset, model version, configuration, and evaluation procedure.
2. **Be transparent:** Publish methodology and explain important limitations.
3. **Be evidence-based:** Distinguish measured results from assumptions or claims.
4. **Respect data rights:** Verify dataset licences, usage restrictions, privacy requirements, and consent where applicable.
5. **Represent language diversity:** Avoid assuming that results for one language generalise to all Indian languages.
6. **Use appropriate metrics:** Select metrics that fit the task and supplement automated metrics with human evaluation when necessary.
7. **Avoid misleading rankings:** Explain evaluation scope and uncertainty instead of presenting a single score as a universal measure of model quality.

## What an Evaluation Record Should Include

- Evaluation objective and research question
- Language or languages covered
- Task definition
- Dataset name, version, provenance, and licence
- Model name and version
- Evaluation configuration and software dependencies
- Metrics and scoring methodology
- Results and comparison conditions
- Known limitations and potential sources of bias
- Reproduction instructions
- Date of evaluation

## Current Status

This directory establishes the initial principles and scope for future work.

No official Bharat AI Commons benchmark suite or validated comparative leaderboard is being claimed at this stage.

The initial priority is to identify a narrow, useful evaluation task and document a reproducible methodology before publishing results.

## Future Work

Potential next steps include:

- Reviewing existing Indian-language benchmarks
- Identifying underrepresented languages and evaluation gaps
- Developing a small, reproducible pilot evaluation
- Publishing transparent results with limitations
- Collaborating with universities, researchers, and open-source communities

## How to Contribute

During the initial setup phase, proposed benchmark resources and evaluation ideas should be assessed against the principles above. Please review the repository's [contribution guidelines](../CONTRIBUTING.md).
